Fermentation Monitor — Temp + Gravity Module Schematic

A sealed, floating device — not a Hub add-on, and not gravity-only. The DS18B20 already on board (needed to temperature-compensate the tilt curve) is a real, independently-useful temperature reading, so this single unit covers both temperature and gravity for anyone who wants both — no separate Hub required. Reuses the Hub's power section (TP4056 + DW01A/FS8205 protection + low-Iq LDO + Q1/Q2-gated battery divider) unchanged — the 37mm housing takes the same 18650, so R9 stays at the Hub's 1.2kΩ — plus an ADXL345 accelerometer for tilt-based gravity estimation. The Hub is still its own product for temp-only or humidity — see MULTI_SENSOR_MODULES.md.

Power-section fix reused unchanged from the Hub — see PCB_DESIGN.md
Temperature is now an on-board MCP9808 (U7), not the DS18B20 this drawing shows. Since the unit is submerged anyway, the sensor never needed to be a probe. The MCP9808 sits on U6's existing I²C bus at address 0x18: ±0.25 °C against the DS18B20's ±0.5 °C, cheaper, and it frees GPIO4 and R3. The catch is mechanical — the board must be thermally bonded to the housing wall at the sensor, because a part reading still internal air is wrong however good its datasheet. Where that isn't practical, a discrete DS18B20 glued to the wall is the fallback and the firmware detects which is fitted. This figure has not been redrawn yet.
Order from BOM_TEMP_GRAVITY.md, not from this drawing. The BOM is the authoritative parts list and includes components this figure doesn't draw: the TP4056 input/output caps (C5, C6), the 3V3 bulk cap (C4), and the ADXL345 decoupling (C10, C11). Designators here match the BOM — in particular R11/R12 are the USB-C CC pull-downs (required, or a USB-C charger never enables VBUS) and R7/R8 are the I²C pull-ups; an earlier revision of this drawing used R7/R8 for both.
Housing is ~37mm × 125mm on an 18650, so R9 is 1.2kΩ (~1A, 0.40C). An earlier revision assumed a 25-32mm vial and a ~350mAh cell, which needed 6.8kΩ and gave only about three weeks per charge. Widening to the commercial precedent (KegLand's RAPT Pill is 37 × 125mm on an 18650) takes that to an estimated ~5 months at the firmware's 15-minute interval. Re-size R9 if you change cells: I_charge ≈ 1200 / R_prog — 1A into a 350mAh cell would be ~2.9C, roughly triple its rating.
This module needs new firmware, not a variant of fermentation_monitor_v1.ino. It posts tilt angle, temperature, and battery as three independent readings — temperature is a real, standalone reading from this unit (not just an internal input), so a customer who wants both temp and gravity only needs this one device, no separate Hub. Gravity/SG itself is not posted directly — it comes from a per-device calibration curve (tilt → SG) fitted from a few reference readings, which is backend/dashboard work, not firmware. See MULTI_SENSOR_MODULES.md.

Why the DS18B20 is potted, not on a connector

The Hub's DS18B20 needs a cable through an enclosure wall because the Hub itself sits outside the fermenter. This module is already fully submerged — every seam is a leak risk. Gluing the sensor against the inside of the housing wall gets thermal contact with the liquid without adding a second penetration alongside the USB-C cap.

Mechanical constraints that decide the BOM, not the other way around

Housing is specified at ~37mm × 125mm, matching the commercial precedent (KegLand's RAPT Pill), rather than the narrower 25-32mm test-tube vial an earlier revision assumed. That width is what lets an 18650 fit, which is the difference between roughly three weeks and roughly five months per charge. PCB width has to clear whatever's left beside the cell.
