Fermentation Monitor — CO₂ Safety Module Schematic

An ambient-room safety monitor, not a fermentation sensor — placed away from the vessel, not inside the sealed headspace (see MULTI_SENSOR_MODULES.md for why). Reuses the Hub's power section, plus a Sensirion SCD41 NDIR CO₂ sensor and a locally-driven piezo buzzer so the alarm doesn't depend on WiFi or the dashboard.

Power-section fix reused unchanged from the Hub — see PCB_DESIGN.md
Placement: the room, not the vessel. Active-fermentation headspace runs near 100% CO₂ — every consumer NDIR sensor, the SCD41 included, saturates well below that. This module belongs in ambient air near the fermentation area, monitoring for dangerous room-level buildup, not inside a sealed airlock. Label the enclosure accordingly so it doesn't end up zip-tied to a carboy by mistake.

Why a local buzzer, not just a dashboard alert

Every other alert in this product line can fail soft — a delayed temperature notification means a slightly-off batch. A CO₂ safety alarm that depends on WiFi being up, Supabase being reachable, and someone actively watching the dashboard has already failed at the one thing it exists for. Fire BZ1 in firmware at a hardcoded threshold, independent of any network round-trip; treat the dashboard-configurable threshold as a secondary, adjustable layer on top of that, not a replacement for it.

Why SCD41 and not a cheap MQ-135

MQ-135-class sensors are unspecific gas/VOC sensors with no real CO₂ calibration — a common mistake for exactly this use case. The SCD41 is a genuine NDIR sensor with a spec'd accuracy range (400-5000 ppm) that covers the ambient-safety use case, and it outputs temperature and humidity for free — no separate BME280 needed on this module.

Battery vs. USB-only

The SCD41 draws meaningfully more current per measurement than a DS18B20 read — duty-cycle it with single-shot low-power mode at a 2-5 minute interval rather than continuous periodic mode. If this unit is going to sit on a shelf near an outlet anyway, running it on continuous USB power and omitting U2-U5, J1's charge circuitry, and the battery entirely is a legitimate simplification — see MULTI_SENSOR_MODULES.md for the full BOM either way.
