Firmware TODO introduced by this hardware revision: GPIO25 (or any free output-capable GPIO) must be driven HIGH immediately before readBatteryVoltage() and LOW right after, with pinMode(25, OUTPUT) added in setup(). Without this change the divider reads ~0V on every cycle — this is not optional, and isn't in fermentation_monitor_v1.ino yet.

Why Q2, not a GPIO wired straight to the PMOS gate

The obvious first draft ties the GPIO directly to Q1's gate with R6 as a pull-up. That's not safe here: a push-pull GPIO driving HIGH wins over a 100kΩ pull-up regardless of what it's fighting, so the "off" state ends up at whatever the GPIO's HIGH level is — 3.3V — not at BAT+. With a freshly-charged cell at 4.2V that's VGS ≈ −0.9V on the PMOS, which is inside the threshold region for parts like the AO3401 rather than solidly off. Q2 (a cheap NPN) turns the GPIO into a low-side switch instead: GPIO HIGH pulls the gate to ~0V through Q2 (strongly on); GPIO LOW/floating leaves R6 alone to pull the gate all the way to BAT+ (Vgs = 0, solidly off, no matter the cell voltage).

Other modules in this product line

Gravity/density and CO₂ don't fit on this board — each has a placement constraint (floating in the liquid; ambient room air) that puts it on its own device instead. See MULTI_SENSOR_MODULES.md for the full rationale, and their own schematics: schematic-gravity-module.html (Tilt/Gravity Module) and schematic-co2-module.html (CO₂ Safety Module) — both reuse this board's power section (TP4056 + DW01A/FS8205 + low-Iq LDO + the Q1/Q2-gated battery divider) as-is.
