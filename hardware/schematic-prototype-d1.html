Prototype wiring — D1 Mini ESP32 + ADXL345 + INR18650

The bench rig, drawn at module level. This is not the custom PCB schematic (schematic-gravity-module.html) — that one shows bare SMD parts you can't breadboard. Every line below is a wire you physically run.

It must be an ESP32 board. "D1 Mini" is sold in both an ESP8266 and an ESP32 version, which look nearly identical and are often listed under the same name. The firmware uses <WiFi.h>, RTC_DATA_ATTR, esp_sleep_enable_timer_wakeup() and a 12-bit 3.3 V ADC — all ESP32-only, so on an ESP8266 it does not compile and the 100k/100k divider would also over-range that board's 1.0 V ADC input. Check the module marking, or what it flashes as in the Arduino IDE.
GPIO numbers, not silkscreen labels. Board revisions and clones print these pins differently (IO4, D4, plain 4). The firmware addresses GPIO numbers; match those. Both the D1 Mini ESP32 and the DevKit V1 break out all five.
On a DevKit V1 this power chain does not work. Its AMS1117 regulator needs >4.4 V in to hold 3.3 V out, and a cell is 4.2 V falling — you get brownouts during WiFi transmit that look like random resets. Either regulate the cell to 3.3 V with an HT7333/MCP1700 and feed 3V3 directly, or use the D1 Mini, whose regulator runs from a cell as drawn. Never feed the raw cell into 3V3 (4.2 V against a 3.6 V maximum), and never have USB connected at the same time as an external 3V3 feed.
