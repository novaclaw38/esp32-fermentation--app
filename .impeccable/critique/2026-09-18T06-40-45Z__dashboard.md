### Design Specificity Verdict

### Overall Impression

The complaint is real, and it's specific: the needle, hub, and track are flat, uniform-width vector geometry with no rendering finish — no taper, no bevel, no dial face enclosing the arc. Nothing is broken (detector clean, zero layout defects, all 4 states/2 sizes render without clipping at any viewport), and the parts that don't carry visual weight — numerals, ARIA, the state-glow system — are genuinely well executed. The gap is narrow but exact: everything that should read as a machined instrument part currently reads as a diagram of one.

### What's Working

1. State-glow system (hue + glow-radius paired, respects prefers-reduced-motion) — severity readable before color even registers, a real accessibility win for colorblind users specifically.
2. Numeral typography and placement — tabular nums prevent digit-width jitter, and the clear-zone geometry (the CY/TRACK_R fix from earlier this session) actually solved a real hub/text collision bug rather than hiding it. Confirmed zero overlap at either size, any state, by Assessment B's screenshots.
3. aria-label/stateAnnouncement construction — a visual gauge's meaning is fully ported to assistive tech, not just its raw number. Most dashboards get this wrong; this one doesn't.

### Priority Issues

**[P1] Needle and hub read as flat vector lines, not machined parts**
- Location: dashboard/src/components/Gauge.tsx:210-219 (needle), :222 (hub)
- Why it matters: DESIGN.md itself calls the needle "the moving gauge" — it's the single most load-bearing visual element on the surface, and it's currently the thinnest, flattest thing on the dial: a uniform 3px line with no taper, over a hub that's a flat 6px filled circle with only a drop-shadow for depth. This is the concrete, nameable reason the complaint lands.
- Fix: Taper the needle (wider at the hub, narrower at the tip, or a two-tone fill). Give the hub a radial gradient or a thin inset ring to fake a bezel. Keep the existing glow, add a highlight.
- Suggested command: /impeccable bolder

**[P1] No dial face or bezel — the arcs float directly on the card background**
- Location: dashboard/src/components/Gauge.tsx (track/tick paths, no filled disc anywhere behind them)
- Why it matters: Without an enclosed face, the object never resolves into an instrument — it stays a diagram of one. This is core to reading as generic/cheap rather than premium, independent of the needle/hub issue above.
- Fix: Add a subtle filled circle behind the track — a radial gradient from panel-950 to panel-900, or a faint vignette — before the track path renders.
- Suggested command: /impeccable bolder

**[P2] Redline zones are functionally invisible at sm size**
- Location: dashboard/src/components/Gauge.tsx — the approach-threshold arcs, 3px stroke at 35% opacity against a 10px track
- Why it matters: A declared design feature ("static reference showing where this device's own thresholds sit") delivers nothing at the 112px size used in the device grid — the surface most people actually scan day to day. Confirmed in Assessment A's 3x crop: you have to know to look for it.
- Fix: Either raise width/opacity enough to read at 112px, or drop it at sm deliberately rather than shipping dead visual weight.
- Suggested command: /impeccable bolder

**[P2] endLabels crowd the arc ends at sm size**
- Location: dashboard/src/components/Gauge.tsx:191-213 — fixed 15px SVG font regardless of the size prop
- Why it matters: The one detail the design system claims as product-specific (real hydrometer notation) is the one element that visually breaks under its own weight at the size it's actually used most. Undercuts the system's own stated differentiator.
- Fix: Scale endLabels font size with the size prop (e.g. 11px at sm), and/or push the label radius outward proportionally.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P3] Tick marks have no major/minor hierarchy or graduation**
- Location: dashboard/src/components/Gauge.tsx — six identical 2px lines, evenly spaced
- Why it matters: A real gauge's authority comes partly from a readable scale. Six unlabeled dashes reads as decorative rather than instrumental, compounding the other findings.
- Fix: Differentiate tick weight/length between start/mid/end at minimum, or accept the current minimal choice deliberately and compensate with the face/needle fixes above.
- Suggested command: /impeccable bolder

### Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User):** The sm grid is scannable for color/state, but the redline-zone signal she'd want for a fast "how close to the edge" read is invisible at that size — she has to read the numeral instead, defeating part of the gauge's purpose for exactly the workflow (scanning many devices fast) it's meant to serve.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User):** The ARIA work is genuinely ahead of most dashboards. Two real gaps: "offline" gives no reason why (dead sensor vs. asleep vs. never-provisioned all announce identically), and the tick marks / redline zones are purely decorative SVG with zero text equivalent — a screen-reader user loses the "how close to threshold" signal that a sighted user gets (barely, per the P2 above) from the redline zones. No parity fallback like "12% from max" exists in the aria-label.

**First-time user (no fermentation background):** The dial genre reads instantly (everyone recognizes a speedometer), but the gravity endLabels' hydrometer notation ("0.990"–"1.120") means nothing without domain knowledge — no tooltip or legend explains it, so the one detail meant to reassure a knowledgeable brewer is invisible to exactly the audience it's aimed at.

### Minor Observations

- InstrumentMark (the sign-in wordmark) is proportionally better-crafted than Gauge at the same grammar — a smaller needle-to-track ratio at 40px accidentally reads less flimsy than the same ratio blown up to 224px.
- GaugeTileSkeleton previews layout correctly but previews none of the craft gaps above — first paint sets an expectation the real gauge then undercuts once it loads.

The system explicitly forbids a second accent hue and forbids sizes beyond sm/lg — is that constraint actually blocking the fixes above (bezel, tapered needle, tick scale), or are they fully achievable inside the existing token set, meaning this is pure execution debt rather than a systemic limit?
